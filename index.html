01 · Architecture

One chestpiece. Two independent paths.

Audio and analysis travel separately. The earbuds never wait on the dock, and the dock never depends on a phone, so a busy analysis task cannot stall the sound you are listening to.

Audio path → earbuds

A dedicated real-time pipeline: I2S samples move by DMA into a ring buffer and straight to the radio queue, with no intermediate copying. Short frames keep delay bounded. Target: under 40 ms to ordinary wireless earbuds.

Telemetry path → dock

On a second core, the envelope, S1/S2 timing, heart rate and murmur flags are extracted and broadcast as small connectionless 2.4 GHz frames (for example ESP-NOW). No pairing handshake, no Wi-Fi association.

Autonomous by design

The dock boots straight into a firmware loop and listens for its chestpiece. No smartphone, no companion app, no cloud login. All analysis stays on the device.

02 · Acoustic Engine

Filter before you digitise.

A high-pass stage ahead of the converter rejects sub-10 Hz hand tremor and respiratory baseline wander, so the converter's range is spent on sound, not motion. Try the live simulation: switch the filter off and watch the baseline drift.

04 · Clinical Workflow

Zero bedside friction.

Five moves from pocket to decision, with no device to unlock and no app to open.

Companion-app workflow (common today)
  • Unlock the phone and open the app
  • Pair the stethoscope
  • Look away from the patient to read the screen
Untethered workflow (design intent)
  • Place the chestpiece and hear it in your earbuds
  • Glance at the dock for waveform, rate and flags
  • Nothing to unlock, pair through an app, or sign in to

Grant summary · BIRAC E-YUVA

Untethered Dual-Path Bio-Acoustic Auscultation System

Zero-Tether Auscultation. Dedicated Telemetry. Zero Bedside Friction.

Problem

Bedside auscultation depends on the examiner, is hard in noisy wards, and many digital stethoscopes still need a phone app for any visual feedback. Wireless audio adds delay, and hand motion masks low-frequency sounds.

Solution

A tubing-free chestpiece that streams audio straight to ordinary wireless earbuds while, on an independent path, sending telemetry to an autonomous bedside dock that draws the phonocardiogram, heart rate, S1/S2 markers and murmur flags.

What is new

  • Audio and analysis on separate paths and cores
  • Pre-digitisation rejection of sub-10 Hz motion
  • Silicone-isolated floating transducer
  • Dock needs no phone, app, cloud or Wi-Fi

Status

Concept with documented architecture and a provisional patent filing at the Indian Patent Office, Kolkata. No bench or clinical results yet.

24-month plan

  • M1–6: front-end and dual-path bench prototype
  • M7–12: dock and edge DSP
  • M13–18: ward validation under ethics approval
  • M19–24: design for manufacture, regulatory groundwork

Measures of success

  • Audio latency vs the 40 ms target, across earbuds
  • Signal quality and noise rejection on the bench
  • S1/S2 detection on open datasets
  • Murmur-flag accuracy vs echocardiography

Funding request

Concept-stage project, not a cleared medical device. Latency figures are design targets.

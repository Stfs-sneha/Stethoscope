Specifications

Design targets for the first prototype.

Transducer and isolation

A suspended contact transducer (piezo or MEMS) behind a closed air chamber, held in a silicone isolation member (target Shore 20A to 40A) and wired with a flexible lead, so shell friction never reaches the sensor.

Analog front-end

Active high-pass stage rejecting content below about 10 Hz, followed by gain, all before digitisation.

Sampling

Hardware I2S with DMA at 4 to 8 kHz.

Processor

Dual core: one core for the audio ring buffer, one for feature extraction.

Radios

Audio over BLE Audio (LC3) or A2DP; telemetry over ESP-NOW broadcast at 2.4 GHz.

Form factor

Coin profile, under 40 g, 8 to 15 mm thick.

Latency you can't hear

Audio is streamed on a dedicated core through a zero-copy ring buffer fed by I2S DMA. Telemetry runs on the other core, so analysis spikes never stutter the sound.

At the bedside

  1. Place

    Set the coin on the chest. The ring lights when contact is good.

  2. Listen

    Sound reaches your own earbuds in real time. No pairing with a phone.

  3. Watch

    The dock draws the waveform, rate, S1 and S2, and murmur flags, with no Wi-Fi or login.

  4. Pocket

    Nest the chestpiece in the dock to charge. The whole kit goes in a coat pocket.

Grant dossier

Prepared for the BIRAC E-YUVA call. Lead innovator: Snehangshu Roy, MBBS scholar, All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Kalyani.

The problem

Tube stethoscopes tether the clinician to one ear-to-chest geometry. Digital ones add lag or need a phone, an app and a network.

The innovation

Audio and visual telemetry travel on separate wireless paths, from one pocket-sized, tubing-free device that needs no phone, app, cloud or ward Wi-Fi.

Stage

BIRAC E-YUVA Innovator Scheme, diagnostic instrumentation. Currently TRL 3 (proof of concept), aiming for TRL 4 through bench and laboratory validation.

IP and regulatory

Indian patent application: provisional specification (Form 2), IPO Kolkata Branch. Investigational prototype, not a certified medical device.

Grant summary

A pocket-sized, tubing-free digital stethoscope that sends low-latency audio to the clinician's own earbuds and, on a separate path, live waveform and heart-sound markers to a standalone dock.

Unmet need

Digital stethoscopes add smartphone pairing, an app and a network to the bedside, and consumer wireless audio lags behind the sound.

Solution

Audio streams from a dedicated core straight to the clinician's earbuds, while a second core broadcasts telemetry to an autonomous dock. The transducer is isolated from finger noise by a silicone member and a flexible lead.

Proposed 12-month plan

  • Months 1 to 4: prototype boards and silicone-molded chassis.
  • Months 5 to 8: bench latency tests and acoustic-chamber calibration.
  • Months 9 to 12: supervised clinical evaluation, after ethics approval.
